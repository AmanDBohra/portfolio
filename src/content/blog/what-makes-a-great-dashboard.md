I've built dashboards for over nine years, and the best ones share a few habits that have nothing to do with fancy charts. Here's what actually matters.

## Start with the KPIs, not the charts

A **KPI (Key Performance Indicator)** is one of the *few* numbers that tell you whether you're winning — not every number you *could* show.
*Like the key grades on a report card, not every doodle in the notebook.*

The most common dashboard mistake is putting twenty metrics on one screen. That doesn't make decisions easier — it makes them harder. A great dashboard is ruthless about what it leaves out. Ask of every tile: *"what decision does this change?"* If the answer is "none," cut it.

## A dashboard is a cockpit, not a data dump

A good **dashboard** shows the vital signs at a glance, then lets you drill in for detail.
*Like an airplane cockpit — the critical gauges are front and centre; everything else is one layer down.* Top-level numbers up top, supporting detail below, filters to explore. Structure beats density every time.

## Self-Service BI — teach people to fish

**Self-Service BI** means giving business users tools to answer their own questions without waiting on a technical team.
*A salad bar instead of ordering from the kitchen every time.*

Done well, it's liberating and it scales. Done badly, everyone builds their own conflicting version of "revenue." The secret is a **governed foundation** underneath — one agreed set of definitions (a semantic layer) — so freedom on top doesn't create chaos.

## Data storytelling — the last mile

The best analysis fails if nobody understands it. **Data storytelling** means presenting data as a clear narrative — context, insight, and "so what" — not just charts.
*A good tour guide explains what you're seeing, instead of just pointing at buildings.*

In practice: lead with the headline ("premium is up 8%, driven by two regions"), show the evidence, and end with the decision it supports. Colour and layout should guide the eye to what matters, not decorate.

## The through-line

Great dashboards aren't about more data — they're about **clarity**: the right few KPIs, a structure that mirrors how people decide, a governed base so self-service stays trustworthy, and a story that makes the "so what" obvious. The real deliverable was never the dashboard. It was shared understanding.
