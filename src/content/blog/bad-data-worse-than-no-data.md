No data makes you cautious. **Bad data makes you confident — and wrong.** That's why, given the choice, a wrong number is more dangerous than a missing one: it gets acted on.

Early in my career I watched teams make decisions on numbers nobody had validated. The dashboard looked authoritative, so the discussion moved on. The quiet cost showed up later, in decisions that were built on sand.

## Governance as a habit, not a phase

"Data governance" sounds like a separate initiative with its own steering committee. In practice, the governance that actually works is small, boring, and continuous — built into delivery rather than bolted on after:

- **Maker/checker controls** so no single person's mistake reaches production unreviewed.
- **Validation at the source**, not just at the dashboard, so errors are caught before they propagate.
- **Cleansing and reconciliation** as part of the pipeline, not a quarterly cleanup.

Applying these on enterprise BI engagements is how I **improved data accuracy by 35%** and **reduced audit findings by 15%** — not through a big governance program, but through habits baked into how the data was built.

## The mindset shift

Treat trust as a feature of the deliverable, equal to the chart itself. A beautiful dashboard on unverified data is a liability with good styling. The question isn't only *"is this insightful?"* — it's *"would I stake a decision on this number?"* If the honest answer is no, the analysis isn't done.

Good data is quiet. You only notice it when it's missing — usually right after someone has already acted on the bad version.
