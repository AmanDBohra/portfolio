I've mentored analysts across engagements — from a BI team of ten-plus early on, to cross-functional teams on international insurance work, to rural students as a volunteer AI mentor. The pattern that works is always the same: **mentor toward independence, not dependence.**

## Teach the question, not the click

It's tempting to just show someone the steps — click here, write this join, use that filter. But steps expire. The analyst who understands *why* a star schema makes a report trustworthy, or *why* a KPI has to map to a decision, can figure out the next problem without you. I try to leave people with a better question, not just a finished answer.

## Standards free people up — they don't box them in

Junior analysts often think structure is the opposite of creativity. It's the reverse. When reporting frameworks are standardized and reusable, people stop reinventing plumbing and start spending their energy on the actual problem. Consistency is what let teams I've led improve delivery efficiency and accessibility without burning out — the framework carried the routine so people could focus on the hard parts.

## Feedback is a gift, so give it honestly

The kindest thing you can do for someone growing is tell them the truth early, specifically, and without drama. Vague praise feels nice and helps no one. "This dashboard is cluttered" is useless; "these four tiles don't change any decision — cut them and the story gets clearer" is something they can act on tomorrow.

## Meet people where they are

Mentoring rural students with little prior exposure to AI taught me to strip jargon to zero. If you can't explain a concept in plain language, you don't understand it well enough yourself. That discipline — explaining the complex simply — has made me better with senior stakeholders too, not just beginners.

## The measure of good mentoring

You've mentored well when people stop needing you for the thing you taught them — and come to you for the next, harder thing instead. Growing people who outgrow you isn't a loss. It's the entire point, and it's how delivery scales beyond any one person.

*— Aman Bohra*
