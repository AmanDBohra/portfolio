Insurance runs on a handful of metrics. Once they click, most insurance dashboards make sense. Here they are without the actuarial jargon — the ones I work with daily leading an international P&C module.

## Written vs. Earned Premium

- **Written Premium** — the total premium on policies *sold* in a period, before anything is deducted. It measures new business volume.
- **Earned Premium** — the portion of that premium for coverage the insurer has *already provided.*

*Pay a full year's gym membership upfront: the whole amount is "written" today, but the gym only "earns" it month by month as you use it.* If you cancel in month three, nine months were written but never earned.

Why it matters: written premium tells you about sales momentum; earned premium is what you compare against claims to judge profitability.

## Loss Ratio

**Loss Ratio = claims paid ÷ earned premium.** It's the single clearest read on underwriting health. Lower is better.
*Spending vs. income — you want to pay out less than you take in.* A 60% loss ratio means 60 paise of every earned rupee went back out as claims.

## Combined Ratio

Claims aren't the only cost — there are also expenses (commissions, admin). **Combined Ratio = loss ratio + expense ratio.**

- **Below 100%** → the insurer made an **underwriting profit** (took in more than it paid out and spent).
- **Above 100%** → an underwriting **loss** (covered only by investment income, if at all).

*Total costs vs. what you earned — stay under 100% and you kept money on the core business.*

## Distributor / Producer Performance

Insurers sell through agents and brokers ("distributors" or "producers"). Distributor performance measures **how much business each one brings — and how profitable it is.**
*A sales leaderboard, but smarter: not just who sells the most, but whose sales actually pay off* (high volume with a bad loss ratio isn't a win).

This is exactly the reporting I lead: Distributor Performance KPIs built on Written & Earned Premium, which improved reporting accuracy by 25% for international operations.

## The big picture

Put together: **Written** shows momentum, **Earned** anchors profitability, **Loss Ratio** and **Combined Ratio** judge health, and **Distributor Performance** shows where the good (and bad) business is coming from. Master these five and you can read almost any P&C insurance dashboard.
