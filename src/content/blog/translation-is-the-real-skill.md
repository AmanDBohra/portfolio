The hardest part of enterprise analytics isn't building dashboards. It's making sure the **right people trust the right numbers to make the right decisions.**

Over 9+ years and 10+ enterprise clients across insurance, banking, retail, automotive, and pharma, that's the problem I keep solving — in different industries, with different tools, at increasing scale.

## Where analytics quietly fails

A data science model can be brilliant and still change nothing. A dashboard can be technically flawless and still go unused. The failure usually isn't in the analysis — it's in the **handoff**.

- The model outputs a probability; the business needed a recommendation.
- The dashboard shows twenty metrics; the decision hinges on two.
- Two teams have "the same KPI" — and different numbers in different rooms.

None of these are technical failures. They're translation failures.

## What translation actually looks like

Today I lead the International module of a major US P&C insurance engagement. A core part of the job is sitting between data engineers, data scientists, and business stakeholders and making sure the collective output actually **reaches the decision it was meant to inform.**

In practice that means:

- Turning ambiguous business questions into precise analytical requirements
- Deciding what *not* to put on the dashboard
- Making a model's output legible to someone who will never open a notebook
- Establishing one governed source of truth so the numbers stop disagreeing

I did an M.Tech in Data Science at BITS Pilani while working full-time — not to become a data scientist, but to understand the data-science side well enough to **translate it for the business.**

## The thread

The thread through my career isn't a tool or a platform. It's a conviction: **analytics only creates value when someone uses it to make a better decision.** Everything upstream — the pipelines, the models, the dashboards — is in service of that moment. Optimize for it.

*— Aman Bohra*
