Leading the International module of a large US Property & Casualty insurance engagement, I've learned that the hardest part of analytics delivery is rarely the analytics. It's keeping people, priorities, and definitions aligned while everything around you keeps changing.

## Own the outcome, not just the output

A dashboard that ships on time but answers the wrong question isn't a win. The job of a delivery lead is to hold the line on *outcome* — the decision the business is trying to make — and let that pull every other choice into place. When you own the outcome, scope arguments get easier: the question stops being "can we build this?" and becomes "does this change a decision?"

## Requirements are a translation problem

Business stakeholders don't hand you specifications — they hand you problems, often in their own language. Turning "we can't see how distributors are really performing" into a governed KPI suite with Written and Earned Premium metrics is translation work. The teams who do this well spend more time listening than building, and they play the requirement back before a single query is written.

## Coordinate across disciplines, don't just assign tasks

On any real engagement you're coordinating analysts, data engineers, BI developers, and data scientists — each with their own rhythm and constraints. The lead's value isn't dividing work; it's managing the *seams* between people. Where does the data scientist's model output become a BI developer's dashboard feed? Who owns the definition when two teams compute "premium" differently? Those seams are where delivery quietly succeeds or fails.

## Make governance routine, not heroic

Trust in reporting should come from evidence, not habit. Building maker/checker controls and data-quality checks into the delivery flow — rather than bolting them on before an audit — is what let us cut audit findings and keep leadership confident in the numbers. Governance done as routine is invisible. Governance done as heroics means you already have a problem.

## Grow the people, and delivery scales itself

Earlier in my career, leading a BI team of ten-plus, the lesson was the same one I lean on now: the fastest way to improve delivery is to make each person better at it. Standardized frameworks, honest feedback, and mentoring turn a group of individuals into a team whose output doesn't depend on any single hero — including you.

## The through-line

Analytics leadership is mostly alignment: aligning the work to the decision, the teams to each other, and quality to trust. The technology matters, but it's the part that behaves. The people and the priorities are where a leader actually earns their keep.

*— Aman Bohra*
