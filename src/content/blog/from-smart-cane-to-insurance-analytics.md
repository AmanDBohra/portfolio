My career didn't begin with a dashboard. It began with a **smart cane**.

As a Computer Science & Engineering student at **Pune University (2013–2017)**, I interned at **Autonetics Centre**, building an assistive IoT device for visually impaired users — Raspberry Pi, sensors, and an Android app that improved obstacle detection by ~30%. It taught me the lesson that has quietly run through everything since: **technology only matters when it changes what a real person can do.**

## Learning enterprise BI the hard way — Icon Business Solutions (2017–2020)

I joined **Icon Business Solutions** as a BI consultant and grew into a **Senior BI Consultant leading a team of 10+**, mostly in **QlikView**, across banking (DBS Bank), automotive (Nissan, Bajaj Auto), pharmaceutical (Lupin, Abbott), and manufacturing clients.

Two projects defined me here:

- **The 36-hour report.** I inherited a report that took 36 hours to run and hung the server every month. Everyone had accepted it. I didn't think it was a data problem — I thought it was a *design* problem. I re-engineered the ETL and transformation layer, converted a monthly batch into a daily parallel run, and cut runtime to **under 2 hours (–94%)**, with **server load down 85%** and **data accuracy up 35%** — no new hardware.
- **The connector-less SAP integration.** When SAP wouldn't connect to our BI tool, I worked with the ABAP team to build a data-refactoring approach that refreshed the BI application **daily, directly from SAP, with no connector** — delivered under a tight deadline.

I left Icon as someone who could own delivery, not just build screens.

## Becoming an analytics consultant — Fractal Analytics (2020–2022)

At **Fractal Analytics** I moved from BI development into **analytics consulting** — retail dashboards that improved sales-tracking accuracy by 20%, and **market basket analysis (Apriori)** applied to a *supply-chain* problem: analyzing Bill-of-Materials hierarchies to **cut inventory costs by 15%**.

Two decisions here shaped my future. First, I started my **M.Tech in Data Science & Engineering at BITS Pilani (2021–2023)** *while working full-time* — because I wanted to understand data science well enough to **translate it for the business**. My capstone, an FMCG retail sales analysis, used Python and association rules to decode purchasing patterns. Second, I began a deliberate, sustained investment in learning.

## The learning engine — how I built depth (2024–2026)

I treated certifications as a roadmap, not a trophy shelf — each one changed how I think.

**Foundations & BI mastery (2024).** I certified across the full **Qlik** stack — Qlik Sense Business Analyst and Data Architect, QlikView 12 Business Analyst and Data Architect — and proved engineering fundamentals with **HackerRank SQL (Advanced)**. Alongside, I completed 40+ courses in Generative AI, machine learning, NLP, and business strategy.

**Modern platform & governance (2026).** I added **Microsoft Power BI Data Analyst Associate (PL-300)**, then moved into the modern data stack with **Databricks** — first the **Data Engineer Associate** and **Generative AI Engineer Associate**, then the **Data Analyst Associate** and **Associate Developer for Apache Spark**, and finally the **Databricks Certified Data Engineer Professional**.

That progression — BI tools → cloud data engineering → Spark → GenAI → the professional engineering cert — mirrors exactly how my *work* was evolving. **Eleven professional certifications in total**, each verifiable.

## Leading at scale — LTIMindtree (2022–Present)

Today I'm a **Senior Analytics & BI Consultant and International Module Lead** at **LTIMindtree**, on a major **US Property & Casualty insurance** engagement. I own analytics delivery end to end — requirements, data engineering coordination, BI development, **data-science translation**, governance, and production support.

I delivered **Distributor Performance Management KPIs** (Written & Earned Premium), improving reporting accuracy by 25%; drove automation that improved process efficiency by 30%; and reduced audit findings by 15%. Much of my day is coordinating analysts, engineers, and data scientists across time zones — and making sure their output reaches the decisions it was meant to inform. I was recognized as a **Star Performer** and served as **Data Steward Leader** for the international portfolio.

## Giving back

Learning compounds when it's shared. I volunteer as an **AI Mentor with eVidyaloka × Microsoft (BRAIN — Build Rural AI Network)**, delivering 37 live sessions to rural students, and mentored a team to **runner-up at the national BRAINIAC 2025 AI competition**. **Terna College of Engineering** invited me to speak on BI trends and advise on their AI curriculum.

## The thread

Across the smart cane, the 36-hour report, the SAP workaround, the market-basket insight, the M.Tech, the eleven certifications, and the Travelers module, there's one conviction underneath:

**Analytics only creates value when someone uses it to make a better decision.**

Everything upstream — the pipelines, the models, the dashboards, the credentials — is in service of that moment. My career has been a steady climb from *building* analytics to *owning* outcomes, and from BI into data engineering, data science, and Generative AI. The tools keep changing. The job stays the same: turn complexity into clarity, and clarity into decisions.

*— Aman Bohra*
