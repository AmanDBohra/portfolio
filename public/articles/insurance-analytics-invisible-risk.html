Insurance Analytics: Making Invisible Risk Visible

Insurance analytics isn't really about dashboards. It's about making invisible risk visible — and legible enough that someone acts on it.

2026-02-25 · by Aman Bohra

  • Insurance Analytics
  • Business Intelligence
  • KPIs

Insurance analytics isn't really about dashboards. It's about making invisible risk visible — and making it legible enough that someone actually acts on it.

Leading the International module of a major US P&C insurance engagement, most of my work lives in a few core numbers: Written Premium, Earned Premium, distributor performance, loss and combined ratio, retention. Individually they're simple. The hard part is making them trustworthy and consistent across regions, teams, and time zones — because a KPI that two teams calculate two different ways isn't a KPI, it's an argument.

The three things that actually make insurance KPIs useful

1. One governed definition. Written vs. earned premium, how a distributor's performance is scored, what counts in a loss ratio — these need a single, documented definition everyone reports against. Without it, every review meeting starts by reconciling numbers instead of making decisions.

2. Reporting built for scrutiny. Insurance is regulated. Reporting that can't survive an audit isn't finished. Building data-quality controls and governance in from the start — rather than bolting them on — is what let me reduce audit findings by 15% while improving reporting accuracy by 25% on distributor KPIs.

3. International context. The "same" metric behaves differently across markets — currencies, regulations, product mixes, seasonality. International analytics is less about translating language and more about translating context so leadership can compare like with like.

The point

The dashboard is the last mile. The value is upstream: consistent definitions, governed data, and context that makes a number mean the same thing in every room. Do that, and the invisible becomes visible — and, finally, actionable.
